# Parallel-coupled section

The building block of parallel-coupled filters: two strips side by side a narrow gap apart over
a quarter wavelength, the first coming from port 1 and ending open, the second beginning open
and running on to port 2. Energy crosses from one strip to the other only through the coupling:
at the quarter wave the section is an impedance inverter of half the difference of the even
and odd impedances, so |S21| there measures the coupling itself. The line theory takes Cohn's
even and odd impedances of the strips as built and the open ends' extension; the strips lie
midway between the planes, so the half below them is meshed under a magnetic wall.

Each open end lies beside the other strip, which runs on past it: its fringing field couples
there too, so the coupled length grows by the extension at both ends. A first trial that took
one end's extension only found the measured response about 3 % lower in frequency than the model.
